Of Suitcases and Forgotten Shores
{: .subtitle}

How much can we carry for others before we realize we've left our own behind?

Beyond the great glass windows of an airport, planes rise into the morning sky and disappear into clouds glowing with the first light of day, while inside, thousands of journeys unfold at once. Some travelers carry little more than a small bag, others drag enormous suitcases filled with everything they cannot bear to leave behind, and above the crowds the departure boards flicker with promises of places yet unseen, every gate offering a possibility, every departure asking for something to be left behind.

![A woman with a backpack and a suitcase watches a plane lift off beyond the airport windows at sunrise, beside a departures board listing Home, Motherhood, Adventure, Belonging, Purpose, Freedom, Connection, A New Beginning and Stillness](/assets/images/featured-the-traveler.jpg){: .photo}

Among them walks a woman who has crossed more borders than she can remember, always ready to help someone find their way, always noticing the lost, the weary, the ones struggling beneath the weight of their luggage. She sees so much. And yet, somehow, no one seems to see her.

* * *
{: .asterism}

Many years ago, she had stood in a terminal facing two very different departures. One gate led toward a world of deep connection, of intuition and invisible currents, of something that seemed to move beneath words and touch, a world where she felt understood in ways she could barely describe, and where she had found a fellow traveler who knew that world as well as she did. She loved that world, and she loved him in it. But beyond that gate the destination was uncertain, and she could not imagine how to build a home there, how to plant roots, how to raise the children she dreamed of, because it seemed to her then that one could not belong to that world and also become a mother.

The other gate led toward a more tangible future: a home, children, a life she could imagine building day by day. She knew which destination she wanted, and yet choosing it meant saying goodbye not only to him, but to the part of herself that belonged to that world.

She accompanied him toward the gate, staying with him for as long as she could, and when he boarded a plane bound for a destination she could not follow, the doors closed, and she listened to the silence that followed. She had known their journeys would separate, had known that the world they shared could not become the life she wanted to build, but knowing did nothing to lessen the pain, and for a while she could not imagine moving toward another gate.

* * *
{: .asterism}

Eventually she boarded the plane toward the life she had chosen, and it carried her across an ocean, away from familiar mountains and languages to a distant shore, where she built a home, became a mother, and filled her days with the beautiful chaos of family life, with laughter and adventures, ordinary routines and extraordinary moments. It was a life she had wanted, a life she loved. And yet there were places within her that her traveling companions did not know how to reach.

When they traveled together, they moved confidently through the terminal, already thinking about their destination, while she followed, pulling her suitcase behind her, quickening her pace when they walked faster, hurrying to catch up when they disappeared around a corner. They had tickets for the same plane, after all, they shared a life beyond the airport… and yet she often found herself traveling alone. Sometimes she tried to show them the quieter landscapes within her, and sometimes they listened, sometimes they smiled, sometimes they simply continued toward the next gate, until eventually she stopped showing them. There was always something else to do anyway, another suitcase to carry, another traveler to help, another departure to organize, and gradually, almost without noticing, she became very good at making sure everyone else arrived safely.

Years passed, then decades, and somewhere along the way she forgot that there had once been another destination she longed to explore.

* * *
{: .asterism}

One day, while traveling with a group, she found herself watching the boarding area. Had everyone arrived? Was anyone missing? Would the slower travelers make it onto the bus? Then she noticed an empty seat beside someone whose company she enjoyed. She imagined the conversation they might have, the laughter they might share, the simple pleasure of traveling together… but the seat near the front would allow her to watch everyone board, so she sat there instead.

One by one the travelers climbed aboard, nobody forgotten, nobody left behind, and the bus departed, and she watched the scenery pass, alone, thinking about that empty seat beside her friend. She had wanted to sit there. So why hadn't she? It wasn't as though anyone had asked her to watch the door, but she had felt responsible for making sure everyone was aboard, just as she felt the urge to help whenever she saw someone struggling with their luggage.

Sometimes travelers welcomed her help, and sometimes they refused, and she found those refusals hard to accept. She had never considered that her eagerness to help might feel like an intrusion. She only knew that when she saw someone struggling, something inside her wanted to reach out, perhaps because she remembered what it felt like to struggle alone, perhaps because she wished someone would reach out to her. But she didn't ask herself those questions yet.

* * *
{: .asterism}

One day, another observant traveler asked her, "Do you feel responsible for the other travelers?"

"No!" she answered, shaking her head, totally unaware of what she had been doing all these years.

He smiled. "Are you sure?"

"Of course!"

And she began explaining, spending an extraordinary amount of time describing how responsible she felt for everyone else, without once realizing that this was exactly what she was doing.

"Perhaps," he said, "there's someone you've forgotten to take care of."

She looked around. "Who?"

He didn't answer right away. Instead, he began walking beside her.

They passed through the terminal together, and for once she wasn't leading anyone toward a gate, nor following someone who walked too quickly, they were simply walking side by side. Passing the security checkpoint felt like entering a safe space, because this other observant traveler didn't need anything from her, he simply was.

She watched an older traveler struggling to lift a suitcase onto the conveyor belt and instinctively moved toward him, but her companion gently asked, "Does he need your help?" She stopped. The older traveler lifted his suitcase (it took some effort, but he managed) and smiled with satisfaction, and the woman smiled too.

"Sometimes, not stepping in means allowing others to discover what they can do themselves," her companion said.

They continued walking. At the next gate she noticed a family rushing toward a departing plane and felt the familiar urge to hurry after them, to make sure they knew where to go, but the family found their way, the plane departed, and the terminal kept moving without her managing it. How strange, how difficult, how liberating… and also, how frightening.

* * *
{: .asterism}

Eventually they arrived at a quieter part of the terminal, where beyond the great windows a narrow bridge stretched toward another part of the airport, and the woman stopped.

"Why are we here?"

"Someone is waiting for you over there," her companion replied.

She looked across and hesitated. The other side seemed like a place she had once known but could no longer quite remember, and something stirred inside her, a distant memory, a faint feeling of home, drawing her toward it without her knowing why.

"Who is it?" she asked.

"Someone you left behind a long time ago."

She had crossed so many bridges, between countries, between languages, between worlds, and yet the middle of a bridge still frightened her, with everything she knew behind her and something she could not yet see ahead. But this time, she wasn't alone.

Her companion walked beside her until they reached the middle, and then he stopped.

"Go sit beside her," he said.

"Aren't you coming?"

He smiled. "I think this part of the journey is yours."

She looked toward the other side, and took another step.

* * *
{: .asterism}

On the far side of the bridge, a woman sat on a bench beside a suitcase. She was not watching the departure board or rushing toward a gate, she was simply sitting there in the soft morning light, and there was something strangely familiar about her.

"Are you waiting for someone?" the traveler asked.

"Yes."

"Did someone leave you behind?"

"Yes."

The traveler felt a sudden ache in her chest. "Who?"

The woman looked at her. "You."

Then the traveler sat down beside her, noticing the sadness in her eyes, and beneath it, a spark, a kind of beauty that seemed timeless. For a long moment neither of them spoke.

The woman opened her suitcase, and inside were dreams, a longing for deep connection, for emotional intimacy, for a spiritual path the traveler had once walked and then set down.

"I thought I'd lost you," the traveler whispered.

The woman shook her head. "No. You left me here."

"Why didn't you follow?"

"There was no room for me."

"I thought I had to choose."

"I know."

"I was building something beautiful."

"I know that too."

The traveler looked at her, tears streaming down her face. "Can I stay with you now?"

The woman reached for her hand. "I've never stopped waiting for you."

And so they sat, crying, hugging, and simply being. The traveler did not know what would happen next, how she could bring these forgotten parts of herself into the life she had built. She wanted them back, but she couldn't imagine where they would fit, and it seemed impossible and inevitable all at once…
