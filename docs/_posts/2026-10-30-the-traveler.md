Of Suitcases and Forgotten Shores
{: .subtitle}

How much can we carry for others before we realize we've left our own behind?

Beyond the great glass windows of an airport, planes rise into the morning sky, disappearing into clouds glowing with the first light of day.

![A woman with a backpack and a suitcase watches a plane lift off beyond the airport windows at sunrise, beside a departures board listing Home, Motherhood, Adventure, Belonging, Purpose, Freedom, Connection, A New Beginning and Stillness](/assets/images/featured-the-traveler.jpg){: .photo}

Inside, thousands of journeys unfold at once. Some travelers carry little more than a small bag. Others drag enormous suitcases filled with everything they cannot bear to leave behind.

Above the crowds, departure boards flicker with promises of places yet unseen. Every gate offers a possibility, every departure asks for something to be left behind.

Among them walks a woman who has crossed more borders than she can remember, always ready to help someone find their way, always noticing the lost, the weary, the ones struggling beneath the weight of their luggage. She sees so much. And yet, somehow, no one seems to see her.

* * *
{: .asterism}

Many years ago, she had stood in a terminal facing two very different departures. One gate led toward a world of deep connection, of intuition and invisible currents, of something that seemed to move beneath words and touch, a world where she felt understood in ways she could barely describe, and where she had found a fellow traveler who knew that world as well as she did. She loved that world, and she loved him in it. But beyond that gate the destination was uncertain, and she could not imagine how to build a home there, how to plant roots, how to raise the children she dreamed of, because she could not imagine how to belong to both worlds at once.

The other gate led toward a more tangible future: a home, children, a life she could imagine building day by day. She knew which destination she wanted, and yet choosing it meant saying goodbye not only to him, but to the part of herself that belonged to that world.

She accompanied him toward the first gate, staying with him for as long as she could, and when he boarded a plane bound for a destination she could not follow, the doors closed, and she listened to the silence that followed. She had known their journeys would separate, had known that the world they shared could not become the life she wanted to build, but knowing did nothing to lessen the pain, and for a while she could not imagine moving toward either gate.

* * *
{: .asterism}

Eventually she boarded the plane toward the life she had chosen, and it carried her across an ocean, away from familiar mountains and languages to a distant shore, where she built a home, became a mother, and filled her days with the beautiful chaos of family life, with laughter and adventures, ordinary routines and extraordinary moments. It was a life she had wanted, a life she loved. And yet there were places within her that her traveling companions did not know how to reach.

When they traveled together, they moved confidently through the terminal, already thinking about their destination, while she followed, pulling her suitcase behind her, quickening her pace when they walked faster, hurrying to catch up when they disappeared around a corner. They had tickets for the same plane, after all, they would arrive at the same destination, they shared a life beyond the airport… and yet she often found herself traveling alone. So she kept walking.

They shared a home, meals, journeys and plans, but when she tried to show them the quieter landscapes within her, they often seemed unable to see them. She tried explaining, she tried inviting them closer, and sometimes they listened, sometimes they smiled, sometimes they simply continued toward the next gate, until eventually she stopped asking so often. There was always something else to do anyway, another suitcase to carry, another traveler to help, another departure to organize, and gradually, almost without noticing, she became very good at making sure everyone else arrived safely.

Years passed, then decades, and somewhere along the way she forgot that there had once been another destination she longed to explore.

* * *
{: .asterism}

One day, while traveling with a group, she found herself watching the boarding area. Had everyone arrived? Was anyone missing? Would the slower travelers make it onto the bus? Then she noticed an empty seat beside someone whose company she enjoyed. She imagined the conversation they might have, the laughter they might share, the simple pleasure of traveling together… but the seat near the front would allow her to watch everyone board, so she sat there instead.

One by one the travelers climbed aboard, nobody forgotten, nobody left behind, and the bus departed, and she watched the scenery pass, alone, thinking about that empty seat beside her friend. She had wanted to sit there. So why hadn't she? It wasn't as though anyone had asked her to watch the door, but she had felt responsible for making sure everyone was aboard, just as she felt the urge to help whenever she saw someone struggling with their luggage.

Sometimes travelers welcomed her help, and sometimes they refused, and she found those refusals strangely difficult to accept, because surely a lighter suitcase would make their journey easier? What she didn't realize was that not everyone wanted someone else to carry their belongings: some had carefully chosen what to pack, some were learning how much they could carry, some needed to discover their own strength, and a few would rather struggle with their suitcase than have a stranger take it from their hands.

The woman had never considered that her eagerness to help might feel like an intrusion. She only knew that when she saw someone struggling, something inside her wanted to reach out, perhaps because she remembered what it felt like to struggle alone, perhaps because she wished someone would reach out to her. But she didn't ask herself those questions yet.

* * *
{: .asterism}

One day, another observant traveler asked her, "Do you feel responsible for the other travelers?"

"No!" she answered, shaking her head. "Absolutely not!"

He smiled. "Are you sure?"

"Of course!"

And she began explaining, spending an extraordinary amount of time describing how she took care of everyone else, without realizing that this was exactly what she was doing.

"Perhaps," he said, "there's someone you've forgotten to take care of."

She looked around. "Who?"

He didn't answer right away. Instead, he began walking beside her.

* * *
{: .asterism}

They passed through the terminal together, and for once she wasn't leading anyone toward a gate, nor following someone who walked too quickly. They were simply walking side by side.

As they passed through security, she noticed she didn't have to hurry, explain, organize, or look after anyone. The traveler beside her asked for nothing. He simply walked with her, and somehow, that was enough for her to feel safe.

She watched an older traveler struggling to lift a suitcase onto the conveyor belt and instinctively moved toward him, but her companion gently asked, "Does he need your help?" She stopped. The older traveler lifted his suitcase (it took some effort, but he managed) and smiled with satisfaction, and the woman smiled too.

"Sometimes, not stepping in means allowing others to discover what they can do themselves," her companion said simply.

They continued walking. At the next gate she noticed a family rushing toward a departing plane and felt the familiar urge to hurry after them, to make sure they knew where to go, but the family found their way, the plane departed, and the terminal kept moving without her managing it. How strange, how difficult, how liberating… and also, how frightening.

* * *
{: .asterism}

Eventually they arrived at a quieter part of the terminal, where a narrow, glass-enclosed pedestrian bridge connected two wings of the airport, and the woman stopped.

"Why are we here?"

"Someone is waiting for you over there," her companion replied.

She looked across and hesitated. The bridge looked narrow, and the other side seemed strangely familiar, like a place she had once known but could no longer quite remember. Something stirred inside her, a distant memory, a faint feeling of home, and although she couldn't explain it, she felt both drawn toward that place and afraid of what she might find there.

"Who is it?" she asked.

"Someone you left behind a long time ago. Someone who has been waiting for you."

She thought about the years of traveling, of caring for others, of being there for everyone, and about the many bridges she had crossed, between countries, between languages, between worlds.

And although she had crossed oceans and continents, the middle of a bridge still frightened her. As she stepped onto it, she felt it sway gently beneath her feet. She instinctively reached for the railing, suddenly aware of the distance between the two sides. Behind her lay everything she knew. Ahead lay something strangely familiar, yet still beyond her sight.

Knowing another shore existed did not make the crossing less frightening.

But this time, she wasn't alone.

Her companion walked beside her until they reached the middle, and then he stopped.

"Go sit beside her," he said.

"Won't you come?"

He smiled. "I think this part of the journey is yours."

She looked toward the other side, loosened her grip on the railing, and took another step.

* * *
{: .asterism}

On the far side of the bridge, a woman sat on a bench beside a suitcase, not watching the departure board, not rushing toward a gate, simply sitting there in the soft morning light, and there was something strangely familiar about her.

"Are you waiting for someone?" the traveler asked.

"Yes."

"Have you been waiting long?"

The woman smiled. "Quite a while."

"Did someone leave you behind?"

"Yes."

The traveler felt a sudden ache in her chest. "Who?"

The woman looked at her. "You."

For a long moment neither of them spoke. Then the traveler sat down beside her, noticing the sadness in her eyes, and beneath it, a spark, a kind of beauty that seemed timeless.

The woman opened her suitcase, and inside were dreams, laughter and longing, things the traveler had once loved but had stopped making room for, memories of feeling deeply connected to the world, of sensing beauty in things that could not be measured, of surrendering to moments without needing to understand them, a longing for closeness, for intimacy, for a deeper path she had once walked and then set down. And there was grief.

"I thought I'd lost you."

The woman shook her head. "No. You left me here."

"Why didn't you follow?"

"There was no room for me."

"I thought I had to choose."

"I know."

The traveler remembered the gate, the goodbye, the plane disappearing into the distance, and she remembered the life she had chosen, the family she had created, the years she had spent caring for everyone else.

"I didn't mean to leave you," she whispered.

"I know."

"I was building something beautiful."

"I know that too."

The traveler looked at her, tears streaming down her face. "Can I stay with you now?"

The woman reached for her hand. "I've been waiting for you to ask."

And so they sat, crying, laughing, and simply being. The traveler did not know what would happen next, whether she would return to the familiar side, explore this one, or discover a way to belong to both, and for once she didn't need to decide. She had spent so many years accompanying others toward their destinations, and now she was learning how to accompany herself.
