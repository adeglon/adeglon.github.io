The photograph is back at the top, full width and tall, which is what it is for - it stopped reading as a hero the moment it became a band under a masthead. Only two things sit on it: Agnès' name, at twice its old size, and the navigation. Both live in the top fifth of the frame, which is open sky - the one part of this picture that holds no detail and so gives type a ground for free. Nothing else is on the photograph at all. That restraint is the result of measuring, not taste. There is no area in this frame both large enough and quiet enough for a sentence at headline size: shallow sky, a lake busy with towns and haze, and one smooth patch of grass about 440px wide before it meets the pines. Every version that put the headline there had to either shrink it or darken the picture to dusk. So the five nouns, the claim and the book are all on the cream below, where they can be as large as they like at full contrast - and the photograph keeps its daylight everywhere below the sky. Agnès' own picture: her sons walking down a ridge above Lake Lucerne. Swiss, deliberately - she is Swiss-American and writes about living between the two - and the only candidate that shows a PATH being walked. {%- endcomment %}
