Full-bleed photograph, type on it - but not the way the early versions did it, which is what kept failing. The lesson from those was never "no type on pictures". It was that type needs a piece of the frame dark enough to hold it, and a flat scrim over the WHOLE picture does not give it one: it greys everything equally, so the photograph loses its light and the letters still sit on clutter. What works is what a film poster does - leave the middle of the frame alone and pour the darkness into the two edges where the type actually is. The sky takes the top band and loses nothing, since it holds no detail; the scrub takes the bottom band, and white letters get real black underneath them. So: masthead in the top band, claim in the bottom band, and the whole middle of the photograph - lake, mountains, haze, the two walkers - untouched. Agnès' own picture: her sons walking down a ridge above Lake Lucerne. Swiss, deliberately - she is Swiss-American and writes about living between the two - and the only candidate that shows a PATH being walked. {%- endcomment %}
